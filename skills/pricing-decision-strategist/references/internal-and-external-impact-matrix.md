# Internal & External Impact Matrix: The CMO vs. CFO Dual-Lens Analysis

Comprehensive executive decision framework evaluating how pricing choices reverberate across internal corporate operations, working capital, and market competitive dynamics.

---

## 1. The CMO vs. CFO Dual-Lens Perspective

Every pricing decision triggers a natural tension between marketing expansion and financial survival. An elite pricing strategy reconciles both:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE CMO (CHIEF MARKETING OFFICER) LENS: "Demand, Adoption & Velocity"  │
│ • "Will this price create sticker shock or friction in ad campaigns?" │
│ • "Does the margin leave enough dollar headroom to pay for CAC/ROAS?"  │
│ • "Does this price position the brand above low-cost copycats?"        │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ THE CFO (CHIEF FINANCIAL OFFICER) LENS: "Cash, Liquidity & Solvency"   │
│ • "What is the cash conversion cycle (CCC) at this price point?"       │
│ • "Can this margin absorb a 15% surge in raw material or toll costs?"  │
│ • "Does each unit sale generate enough contribution margin for SG&A?" │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

When recommending a pricing decision, compare 3 distinct operational paths:

```text
┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐

├──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤

```

## 3. Departmental Ripple Effect (Internal Corporate Impact)

A price change does not happen in isolation. It reshapes every department's operational constraints:

### A. Marketing & Paid Acquisition Impact
- **Headroom Equation**:
  $$\text{Allowable CAC} = \text{Selling Price} - \text{COGS} - \text{Target Operating Profit} - \text{Fulfillment}$$
- *Low Margin (Option 1)*: If Selling Price = Rp 50.000, COGS = Rp 25.000, target profit = Rp 10.000, fulfillment = Rp 8.000; allowable CAC is only **Rp 7.000** (impossible on Meta/TikTok ads; ROAS must exceed 7.0x).
- *Sweet Spot (Option 2)*: If Selling Price = Rp 95.000, COGS = Rp 25.000, target profit = Rp 25.000, fulfillment = Rp 10.000; allowable CAC is **Rp 35.000** (sustainable on Meta ads; viable at ROAS 2.7x).

### B. Sales Team & Channel Distribution Impact
- **Retail & Distributor Margin Stacking**:
  ```text
  Customer Price (SRP): 100% ──► Retailer Margin (30%-35%) ──► Distributor Margin (15%-20%) ──► Factory Net (45%-55%)
  ```
- If your initial markup is only 1.8x COGS, you **cannot enter modern retail (Indomaret, Alfamart, Watson, Guardian)** because distributor and retailer margin cuts will push you into negative net profit. You must price at $\ge 3.0\times \text{ COGS}$ to support multi-tier retail channels.

### C. Finance & Working Capital Impact
- **Inventory Cash Drag**: Low-markup products require 5x the warehouse inventory volume to generate the same gross profit rupiah as high-markup products, trapping precious cash in unsold stock.
- **Inflation Buffer**: High markup ($\ge 65\%$ margin) absorbs supplier price increases without forcing frequent, consumer-alienating price hikes.
